Chapter 01

GOOD MORNING

A) Describe the Song

Good Morning - Kanye West

"Good Morning" is by Kanye West, from the album Graduation (2007). Genre-wise, it fits hip-hop with a bright, anthem-style production. Kanye is known for blending personal storytelling with big ideas about ambition, confidence, and pressure.

In context, this song opens the album and feels like stepping into a new chapter. It sounds like someone waking up, stepping up, and trying to own their future.

Fact check: Most sources list 2007 release with production centered on a sampled melody from Elton John material; exact sample credit details can vary by source.

B) Mood and Message

Upbeat, focused, and nostalgic

The mood feels happy and upbeat, but not random energy. It sounds like momentum. For me it is also nostalgic, like remembering why I wanted to improve in the first place.

The message I hear is: be proud of progress, but still stay hungry. It is not saying life is perfect. It is saying wake up and do the work.

C) Lyric(s) + Personal Connection

"Good morning, look at the valedictorian"

This lyric connects to me because I do not only hear "best student." I hear "best version of yourself." That matches my values: growth, creativity, and uniqueness.

I learn fast and think outside the box, but I also overthink and procrastinate when I should just start. This lyric pushes me to stop waiting for perfect conditions and build consistency. I want momentum, not excuses.

D) Now vs Future

Both now and future

This song reflects how I live now and how I want to live later. Right now, I am growing and trying to become more consistent. In the future, I want that consistency to be automatic, not forced.

The valedictorian line reminds me that success is not one final title. It is daily habits. My focus is to keep starting faster, finish more often, and build confidence through action.

Bridge

4/4 FEEL

Good Morning gives me drive. Take Five shows me how subtle originality can still be powerful. This next chapter is about being different with purpose, not noise.

Chapter 02

TAKE FIVE

A) Describe the Song

Take Five - The Dave Brubeck Quartet

"Take Five" is performed by The Dave Brubeck Quartet and written by Paul Desmond. It is a jazz standard from the Time Out era (commonly dated to 1959 recording/release). Genre: cool jazz with experimental rhythm choices.

The context was exploring unusual time signatures while still sounding accessible. Instead of making "weird" music for shock value, the group made something clean, memorable, and intentional.

Fact check: Most references place recording/release in 1959, while some popular history notes major chart momentum around 1961.

B) Mood and Message

Calm confidence and precision

The mood is cool, focused, and controlled. It never feels chaotic, even though the rhythm is uncommon. That balance is the message I take from it: different can still be clear.

It sounds like creativity with restraint. Every part has space, and that space makes the details matter more.

C) Musical Elements + Personal Connection

5/4 rhythm: familiar first, different when you count

"Take Five" can feel like 4/4 until you really count it. That matches how I work. At first my work can look standard, but if you look closer, the difference is in the choices and effort behind it.

This is "different done well." I do not want to do too much just to look unique. I want to use limited tools intentionally and make something strong. That reflects my creativity and originality in a subtle way.

D) Now vs Future

Mostly now, and also a future standard

This song mostly reflects how I already think now: I appreciate subtle creativity and originality. I like when ideas are smart, not loud.

For the future, it sets a standard: keep making work that looks clean on the surface, but has depth underneath. I want people to notice the quality more and more the closer they look.

Why These Two Together

Lyrics + structure = my growth formula

"Good Morning" gives me direct motivation through lyrics and energy. "Take Five" gives me a design mindset through structure and restraint. One pushes momentum. The other protects quality.

Together they represent who I am building: someone who starts faster, stays consistent, and keeps originality intentional instead of forced.

Unit Reflection

Prompts + Starter Plan

Prompt 1: What did I learn about identity? I learned my identity is not only personality traits. It is also habits. If I want growth, I need actions that match my values.

Prompt 2: How did music help me reflect? Lyrics gave me direct meaning, and musical structure gave me a new way to think about how I create.

Prompt 3: What is one change I will keep? I will start tasks earlier, even with an imperfect first draft, so overthinking does not block progress.

Starter plan (next 2 weeks):

  1. Use a 20-minute start rule for homework and projects.
  2. Track consistency instead of waiting for perfect results.
